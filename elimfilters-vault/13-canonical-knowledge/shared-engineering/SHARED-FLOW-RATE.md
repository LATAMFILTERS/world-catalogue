## Technical Relationships — Validated

- Oil-filter protection depends on balancing particle capture efficiency, contaminant-holding capacity and lubricant flow rather than optimizing a single parameter. | status: approved
- A micron value does not define filtration performance unless it is paired with capture efficiency at that particle size. | status: approved
- Longer lubricant drain intervals increase the time the filter remains exposed to contaminant loading, pressure cycling and thermal stress. | status: approved
- Lubricant chemistry alone does not determine filter compatibility; service interval, application requirements and filter durability must also be considered. | status: approved
- Excessive pressure difference across the filter can trigger bypass operation so lubricant flow can continue around the restricted media. | status: approved
- During bypass operation, lubrication flow may continue while filtration through the main media is temporarily reduced or absent. | status: approved
- A bypass setting must be treated as application-specific rather than as a universal filter value. | status: approved
- Physical similarity does not establish functional interchangeability between oil filters. | status: approved
- Correct fitment requires compatible sealing, attachment geometry and application-specific flow and pressure-control requirements. | status: approved
- Oil-filter architecture combines the filtration element with structural, sealing and flow-control components whose functions depend on the application. | status: approved
- Spin-on and cartridge designs package these functions differently even when both perform engine-lubricant filtration. | status: approved
- Lower lubricant temperature generally increases viscosity, which can increase resistance to flow during cold-start conditions. | status: approved
- Cold-start flow demand can increase pressure differential across the filter and make correct application-specific flow and bypass characteristics important. | status: approved
- Engine-air-filter designs must provide particle capture while maintaining acceptable airflow and sealing within the intake housing. | status: approved
- Media construction and filter geometry affect contaminant capacity, restriction behavior and fitment. | status: approved
- Layered cabin-filter media can be designed for a specific airflow direction, so installation orientation is a functional requirement rather than a cosmetic choice. | status: approved
- The cabin filter removes airborne particulate matter from air entering the passenger-compartment HVAC path. | status: approved
- As contaminant loading increases, airflow through the element can decline and service replacement becomes necessary. | status: approved
- Activated-carbon cabin media adds adsorption capability for selected gases and odors while the filter structure continues to manage particulate contamination. | status: approved
- Adsorption capacity is finite and should be treated as a service-life property rather than a permanent function. | status: approved
