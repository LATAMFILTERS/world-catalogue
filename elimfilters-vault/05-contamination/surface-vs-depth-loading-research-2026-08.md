# Surface Loading vs. Depth Loading — Internal Research Synthesis

**Date:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** INTERNAL EVIDENCE — NOT PUBLIC CLAIM LANGUAGE  
**Use:** Knowledge enrichment, engineering review, future source validation

## Scope

This note records externally derived research supplied for internal ELIMFILTERS knowledge enrichment. It distinguishes general filtration principles from technology-specific claims that must not be published as ELIMFILTERS performance statements without independent validation.

## General engineering principles that may be used publicly

### Depth loading

Depth-loading media captures and retains contaminant through a meaningful portion of the media thickness. Particle penetration, pore structure, fiber arrangement, fluid velocity and contaminant characteristics determine how the loading profile develops.

Engineering consequences can include:

- contaminant storage distributed through media depth;
- progressive change in differential pressure as pores and internal pathways load;
- service behavior strongly dependent on media structure, available area and contaminant distribution;
- limited recoverability by surface cleaning when contaminant is embedded within the media structure.

### Surface-oriented loading

Surface-oriented media is designed so a greater share of contaminant remains near the upstream face or a fine upstream layer instead of penetrating deeply into the supporting structure.

Engineering consequences can include:

- reduced deep penetration of captured particulate;
- a different pressure-drop development curve compared with depth-oriented structures;
- easier removal of accumulated surface dust in applications specifically designed for regenerative cleaning;
- performance that depends on fiber structure, contaminant characteristics, face velocity, support substrate and cleaning method.

### Pressure drop and energy

Pressure drop is not determined by loading mechanism alone. It is affected by media permeability, fiber diameter, basis weight, pleat geometry, available filtration area, fluid properties, velocity and contaminant loading. In fan-driven air systems, higher system resistance can increase fan energy demand depending on the fan/control architecture.

### Application boundary

Surface-loading benefits observed in pulse-cleaned industrial dust collectors must not be generalized automatically to engine intake filters, fuel filters, hydraulic filters, cabin filters or other non-regenerating filtration circuits.

Different circuits use different fluids, contaminant distributions, flow regimes, pressure ranges, media structures and service strategies.

## Externally derived technology-specific evidence — internal only

The supplied research describes Donaldson Ultra-Web as an electrospun nanofiber technology using an extremely fine fiber layer over a support substrate. Reported claims supplied in the research include:

- electrospun fibers reported around 0.2–0.3 µm in diameter;
- surface-oriented dust loading;
- improved release of dust during compressed-air pulse cleaning;
- MERV 14 / MERV 15 references under ASHRAE 52.2 for certain industrial-air applications;
- lower operating pressure-drop claims in dust-collection duty;
- reported service-life improvements up to approximately two times versus some conventional cellulose media.

These points are **not ELIMFILTERS claims** and must not be copied into MACROCORE™, SYNTAPORE™, NANOFORCE™, SYNTRAX™ or any other public technology page without validated product-level evidence.

## Public-language rule

Acceptable public language:

> Surface-oriented and depth-loading media represent different contaminant-management behaviors. Their effect on efficiency, contaminant capacity and pressure-drop development depends on the media structure, fluid, contaminant and application.

Unacceptable without ELIMFILTERS validation:

- universal nanofiber diameter claims;
- electrospinning claimed as an ELIMFILTERS manufacturing process;
- MERV / ASHRAE ratings applied to unrelated circuits;
- universal energy-savings percentages;
- universal two-times filter-life claims;
- pulse-cleaning behavior applied to filters not designed for regenerative cleaning.

## Knowledge relationships

- Surface loading ↔ upstream contaminant retention
- Depth loading ↔ contaminant distribution through media thickness
- Media architecture ↔ efficiency
- Media architecture ↔ contaminant-holding capacity
- Media architecture ↔ differential pressure development
- Differential pressure ↔ energy demand in fan-driven systems
- Cleaning strategy ↔ recoverable vs non-recoverable loading
- Application boundary ↔ air / fuel / hydraulic / lube / cabin circuits

## Relevant ELIMFILTERS public contexts

- MACROCORE™: use only as a neutral media-architecture principle tied to engine-air application validation.
- SYNTAPORE™: use only the broader principle that media structure influences particulate retention, contaminant capacity, fuel flow and pressure drop. Do not imply pulse cleaning or dust-collector behavior.
- Knowledge Center: suitable for a general engineering reference explaining surface-oriented loading vs depth loading and the importance of application boundaries.
