NANOFORCE is a sub-micron lube oil and hydraulic fluid filtration technology engineered to achieve ISO 4406 cleanliness codes of 15/13/10 or better by capturing particles in the 1–5 µm size range that pass through conventional full-flow filters. Standard full-flow lube filtration (including high-performance synthetic media) typically achieves Beta efficiency at 10–18 µm absolute — meaning particles below 10 µm pass through and recirculate in the oil circuit. These sub-10 µm particles are the primary wear agents at precision clearances: crankshaft journal bearing hydrodynamic films operate at 15–50 µm clearance, meaning particles in the 1–15 µm range are the dominant abrasive threat. NANOFORCE addresses this gap through nanofiber-enhanced filtration media achieving Beta(x[c]) ≥ 1000 at 5 µm absolute — a removal ratio that reduces sub-micron particle counts by three orders of magnitude per pass through the element.

The technology is deployed as a kidney-loop or bypass filtration circuit operating in parallel with the main full-flow lube filter. This architecture allows NANOFORCE to process a fraction of total oil flow (typically 5–15% of main circuit flow) at very high efficiency and low flow rate — conditions that enable fine media with sub-micron pore structures to operate without excessive pressure differential or bypass valve activation. The kidney-loop configuration means NANOFORCE progressively polishes the entire oil volume over multiple engine operating cycles, reducing the ISO 4406 cleanliness code from a full-flow system baseline of 16/14/11 to 15/13/10 or better. At ISO 4406 15/13/10, bearing fatigue life increases 5–8× compared to commodity full-flow filtration alone at 19/17/14 — the difference between a component lasting one overhaul interval versus five or more intervals.
