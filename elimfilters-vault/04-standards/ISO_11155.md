ISO 11155 is the primary international performance standard for air filters used in the passenger compartments and operator cabins of road vehicles. The standard is published in two parts: ISO 11155-1 covers particle filtration efficiency and pressure drop (airflow resistance) measurement; ISO 11155-2 covers the filtration efficiency of activated carbon layers against gaseous contaminants including odour compounds and specific chemical species. ISO 11155-1 testing uses a standardised airflow rate and synthetic dust challenge, measuring particle capture efficiency at the PM10 and PM2.5 size fractions — the particle size ranges corresponding to inhalable and respirable health fractions defined by ISO 7708 and WHO air quality guidelines. Pressure drop across the filter element is measured simultaneously to confirm that efficiency targets are met without unacceptable restriction of cabin HVAC airflow. ISO 11155-2 testing uses a challenge gas mixture to assess activated carbon layer removal efficiency for odour and chemical contaminants relevant to road vehicle operating environments, including aromatic hydrocarbons and NOx species.

Cabin air filtration in road vehicles and industrial equipment is an operator health protection function, not a comfort feature. Respirable particle exposure in operator cabins — particularly PM2.5 and below — is associated with occupational respiratory disease in industries where diesel exhaust particulate, silica dust, and agricultural bioaerosols are present in the ambient operating environment. ISO 11155-1 particle efficiency testing provides the measurement basis for classifying cabin filters by their protective capacity against PM10 and PM2.5 fractions: filters meeting >80% PM10 efficiency provide baseline inhalable particle protection; filters achieving >60% PM2.5 efficiency provide respirable particle protection relevant to occupational exposure limit compliance. In mining, construction, and agricultural applications where ambient PM2.5 concentrations may exceed 150–500 µg/m³ during active operations, ISO 11155-compliant cabin filtration reduces in-cab concentrations to below WHO 24-hour mean guidelines of 15 µg/m³ — the threshold at which chronic respiratory disease risk begins to accumulate in long-term exposure populations. The standard applies to all road vehicles including buses, coaches, trucks, agricultural tractors, and construction equipment with enclosed operator stations, making [[ISO_11155|ISO 11155]] the universal performance specification for operator cabin air quality management across heavy industry sectors.

ISO 11155 certification of a cabin air filter does not guarantee maintained performance in service — particle capture efficiency degrades as the filter media loads with accumulated dust and biological material, and airflow resistance increases until HVAC fan capacity is reduced below design intent. Replacement interval management based on operating environment severity is a practical operational requirement independent of the ISO 11155 rating, which establishes initial performance rather than service life.
