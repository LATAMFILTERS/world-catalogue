ISO 12937 specifies the coulometric Karl Fischer titration method for determining the water content of petroleum products, including diesel fuel, fuel oil, lubricating oil base stocks, and other petroleum-derived fluids. The method operates on the same electrochemical principle as [[ASTM_D6304|ASTM D6304]] — generating iodine coulometrically at a platinum electrode and reacting it stoichiometrically with water present in the sample — and produces equivalent results to ASTM D6304 when applied to the same sample materials. ISO 12937 is the preferred reference method in European and international markets, where EN 590 diesel fuel specifications, marine fuel standards under ISO 8217, and international regulatory frameworks cite ISO 12937 as the applicable water content test; ASTM D6304 is the North American market equivalent. Both methods measure total water content — dissolved and free water — expressed as mg/kg (parts per million by mass), with sensitivity sufficient to quantify water concentrations in the 10–10,000 mg/kg range relevant to fuel quality management and contamination detection.

The distinction between ISO 12937 and ASTM D6304 is primarily one of market jurisdiction and regulatory reference rather than technical methodology. EN 590, the European standard governing automotive diesel fuel quality, specifies a maximum water content of 200 mg/kg measured by ISO 12937; ISO 8217, the international marine fuel standard, also references ISO 12937 for water content determination in marine distillate and residual fuels. In practice, certified laboratories performing both standards produce results within measurement uncertainty of each other on identical samples, and cross-reference between the two standards is explicitly acknowledged in both documents. Fleet operators managing fuel quality internationally — marine operators, multinational mining contractors, and oil and gas producers operating under both European and North American regulatory frameworks — may encounter both standards cited in different compliance documents for the same fleet.

Water contamination in diesel fuel at concentrations above 200 mg/kg (ISO 12937 EN 590 limit) represents an operational risk to common-rail injection systems operating at 1,800–2,500 bar, where free water droplets cause cavitation erosion of injector nozzle tip geometry. ISO 12937 bulk fuel testing in marine engine rooms, remote site diesel storage, and power generation facility fuel management identifies water accumulation from tank condensation cycles and delivery contamination events before concentrations reach injector damage thresholds. The [[TURBOCORE|TURBOCORE]] coalescing fuel filtration technology is performance-validated by comparing ISO 12937 water concentration measurements of unfiltered inlet fuel against filtered outlet fuel, with target outlet concentrations below the dissolved saturation limit of 50–100 mg/kg — the point below which water exists only in dissolved form incapable of causing hydraulic impact damage to injection system components.
