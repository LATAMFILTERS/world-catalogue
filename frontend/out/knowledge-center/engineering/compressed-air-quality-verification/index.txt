3:T47d,Online dewpoint measurement methods for continuous compressed air quality monitoring: (1) Chilled mirror (hygrometer) — mirrors cool until dew condenses; condensation point measured optically; accuracy ±0.1°C PDP; highest accuracy but slow response (1–5 min) and requires clean air free of oil contamination; (2) Capacitance polymer sensor — aluminium oxide or polymer film changes capacitance with water vapour partial pressure; accuracy ±2°C PDP; fast response (10–60 seconds); can tolerate oil contamination up to 0.1 mg/m3; most common online method; (3) Electrolytic (P2O5) sensor — water electrolytically reduced; measures absolute humidity; accuracy ±1°C PDP; high maintenance (sensor contamination); (4) TDLAS (Tunable Diode Laser Absorption Spectroscopy) — measures water vapour absorption at specific laser wavelength; accuracy ±0.5°C PDP; no moving parts; increasing adoption for critical applications. For pharmaceutical and electronics applications (ISO Class 1 water, PDP ≤ −70°C), chilled mirror or TDLAS measurement is required — capacitance sensors have insufficient accuracy at these low dewpoint levels.4:T427,ISO 8573 compliance documentation requirements: (1) Test report — identifying information (site, system, date, operator), sampling point identification, sample conditions (pressure, temperature, airflow), measurement method and instrument identification, instrument calibration reference, results in ISO 8573-1 class units, and comparison to specification; (2) Instrument calibration records — calibration certificate for each measurement instrument used, dated within the instrument's calibration interval; (3) Sample chain of custody — for laboratory-analysed samples, collection record showing: sample collection point, time, volume, preservation, transport conditions, and laboratory receipt; (4) Trend records — consecutive test results in chronological order for each sampling point, enabling trend analysis to detect gradual quality deterioration before action limits are breached. Check all upstream filter restriction indicators; (3) Corrective action — replace failed component (filter element, dryer desiccant, or valve seal); verify repair by repeat quality test at the failed point; (4) Release testing — two consecutive passing tests before returning system to service; (5) Impact assessment — determine what product or process was exposed to out-of-specification air and evaluate impact; pharmaceutical applications require batch review and potential reject/quarantine of affected product; (6) Preventive action — review the root cause to determine whether frequency monitoring, filter replacement intervals, or alarm systems require modification to prevent recurrence.6:T437,Post-annual-maintenance compressed air quality verification: (1) Pre-startup leak test — pneumatic leak test at 1.1× operating pressure with nitrogen or air after all maintenance; check all disturbed connections using soap solution or ultrasonic leak detector; (2) Purge and verify — after startup, run compressor for minimum 30 minutes to stabilise before taking quality samples; purge all sampling points for 2 minutes before taking samples; (3) Full-parameter verification — test all ISO 8573 parameters (particles, water, oil) at all critical points of use; single-point verification of a central header does not confirm quality at branch ends; (4) Microbiological sampling — post-shutdown system disturbance can introduce biological contamination from opened connections; microbiological test required for pharmaceutical applications after any system maintenance; (5) Instrument verification — verify calibration of all online dewpoint and oil monitoring instruments against reference standards after maintenance; confirm alarm setpoints are correctly configured.7:T41b,ISO 8573-7:2003 defines the method for measuring viable microbiological contamination (bacteria and mould) in compressed air. Two sampling methods are specified: an Anderson Cascade Impactor (for size-resolved viable aerosol characterisation) and an AGI-30 all-glass impinger (for total viable count). For routine ISO 8573-1 class verification, the impinger method is most commonly used. The AGI-30 impinger collects air samples into a sterile liquid collection medium at 12.5 L/min; minimum sample volume is 1,000 L (approximately 80 minutes at 12.5 L/min). After sampling, the collection medium is diluted in serial dilutions and plated on blood agar growth medium; plates are incubated at 37°C ±1°C for 48 hours before colony counting. Results are expressed in Colony Forming Units per cubic metre (CFU/m³). ISO 8573-7 microbiological purity classes: W1 ≤0.1 CFU/m³, W2 ≤1 CFU/m³, W3 ≤10 CFU/m³.
