3:T485,ISO 16889 requires simultaneous upstream and downstream concentration measurements at fixed intervals of cumulative dust injection — typically every 2 g or 5 g of test dust injected. The measurement frequency is critical because: (1) At high measurement frequency, the Beta ratio time series captures efficiency variation across loading states — showing the initial low-efficiency phase (before cake formation), the peak efficiency phase (established cake), and the declining phase near terminal restriction; (2) The standard Beta ratio value is calculated as the arithmetic mean of all measurement pairs, not the peak value — averaging over all loading states provides a conservative estimate; (3) High-frequency measurement enables calculation of Beta stability — the variability of Beta across loading states — which is a supplementary specification for servo valve circuit elements that must maintain consistent efficiency at all loading states. The ISO 16889:2022 revision added Beta stability reporting requirements in response to documented cases where elements showed acceptable average Beta but poor efficiency at specific loading states.4:T40c,ISO 16889:2022 (updated from the 2008 version) includes the collapse pressure test as a mandatory supplementary test for element structural integrity — aligning with the equivalent NFPA T2.14.1 requirement that was previously only specified in the North American market. Test procedure: (1) Pre-test: conduct the multi-pass test to terminal ΔP (6 bar); (2) Apply collapse pressure: reverse the pressure direction (outside-in for elements with normal inside-out flow) to 10× rated operating differential pressure; (3) Hold for 1 minute; (4) Return to forward flow and conduct post-test APC measurement; (5) Acceptance criterion: Beta ratio after collapse test within 10% of pre-collapse Beta ratio; no bypass flow increase; no media visible in downstream flow path. Elements failing the collapse test after reaching terminal restriction have demonstrated that loading the element to service DHC limit leaves inadequate structural margin for pressure transients — these elements must be replaced at a lower DHC threshold in service.5:T453,Both upstream and downstream APCs must be calibrated per ISO 11171 using NIST-traceable particle counting reference material derived from ISO 12103-1 A2 fine test dust. Calibration must be traceable to an ISO 11171-calibrated reference APC; calibration intervals must not exceed 12 months or be exceeded following any service event, impact, or repair to the instrument. Before each test, a coincidence check is performed: a pre-diluted sample of the test slurry is run through both APCs; if the upstream APC records more than 8,000 particles/mL at ≥1 µm(c), the slurry must be diluted until the coincidence threshold is not exceeded, or a dilution circuit must be fitted to the upstream sample line. Coincidence error — the undercounting of particles at high concentration when two or more particles traverse the sensing zone simultaneously — causes artificially low upstream counts and therefore artificially high Beta ratio values. Any test where coincidence correction was not applied at upstream concentrations above 8,000 particles/mL at ≥1 µm(c) is declared invalid per ISO 16889 Section 10.6:T407,Dirt holding capacity (DHC) is measured gravimetrically: the total mass of ISO 12103-1 A2 fine dust injected from test start to terminal differential pressure (6.0 bar across the element, or as specified by the housing manufacturer). DHC = Σ (q_inject × C_slurry × Δt) for all time intervals from test start to terminal ΔP, expressed in grams. The test is terminated when the measured differential pressure across the element reaches the terminal value and remains at or above terminal ΔP for 10 seconds.
