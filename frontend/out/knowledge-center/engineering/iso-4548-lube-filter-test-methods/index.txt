3:T440,ISO 4548-5 provides a single-pass filtration efficiency and retention test specifically for lube filters — distinct from ISO 16889 which is designed for hydraulic elements with a multi-pass circulating test. ISO 4548-5: (1) Single-pass — contaminated oil passes through the element once; efficiency is measured at the element outlet; (2) Test dust — ISO MTD (Medium Test Dust, ASTM A2 fine) at defined concentration; (3) Efficiency reporting — Beta ratio at specified particle size thresholds, similar to ISO 16889 but without the multi-pass contamination accumulation; (4) Dirt holding capacity — measured simultaneously during the efficiency test until bypass valve opens. Single-pass efficiency values from ISO 4548-5 are not directly comparable to ISO 16889 multi-pass Beta ratios — the multi-pass method produces higher average Beta ratios because the filter cake builds during the test, improving efficiency progressively. ISO 16889 is increasingly adopted for lube filter characterisation, making the two datasets incompatible unless test conditions are clearly stated.4:T437,ISO 4548 comprises 13 parts, each addressing a specific performance attribute of full-flow lube oil filters. The most operationally significant parts are: Part 1 (end-load test — axial force resistance); Part 2 (element bypass valve test — cracking pressure and flow characteristics); Part 5 (cold-start simulation — differential pressure at low temperature and high viscosity); Part 6 (static burst pressure — housing pressure containment); Part 7 (fatigue test for filter assemblies — pressure cycling); Part 8 (vibration test — structural integrity under engine vibration); Part 12 (filtration efficiency and dirt holding capacity — multi-pass test); Part 13 (anti-drainback valve test — seal integrity and cracking pressure). Parts 3, 4, and 9–11 address resistance to flow fatigue, high-pressure resistance, uniformity of contamination test, and internal leakage. A complete filter qualification programme references the full set of applicable parts; application-specific parts (e.g. vibration test for engine-mounted filters) are mandatory where relevant.5:T40e,The end-load test (ISO 4548-1) verifies filter assembly resistance to axial forces applied to the element end-caps and housing. During engine operation, a spin-on filter experiences end-load from: (1) system pressure acting on the closed end of the filter (F = P × A, where A is the closed-end area); (2) thermal cycling causing differential expansion between element and housing; (3) mounting orientation — vertically-mounted filters with full oil column weight. ISO 4548-1 applies a controlled axial load to the filter assembly and measures deflection and permanent set. Failure criteria: element bypass path opening; end-cap separation from media; housing deformation allowing housing-to-element bypass. The test load is typically specified as a multiple of the maximum operating pressure load — ensuring a safety factor above normal service conditions. This test is complementary to ISO 2941 (general filter collapse test) but is specific to the spin-on assembly configuration including thread engagement and housing interaction.6:T40c,Part 6 (static burst) pressurises the complete filter assembly to destruction (or to the specified proof pressure), measuring burst pressure and failure mode. For a lube oil filter rated at 10 bar operating pressure, Part 6 typically requires proof at ≥30 bar without leakage. Part 7 (fatigue) cycles the filter between 0 and maximum operating pressure for a defined number of cycles (typically 50,000–100,000 cycles) to simulate long service life under pressure pulsation from the engine oil pump. After the fatigue cycle, the filter is inspected for: media deformation; end-cap bond failure; housing thread wear or fatigue crack; bypass valve seat damage. Fatigue failure mode not detected by static tests: end-cap bond progressive delamination under cyclic Δp — initially a small bypass path that grows cycle-by-cycle.
