THREE-STAGE DEFENSE. FULL CONTAMINATION SPECTRUM.

Conventional fuel filters intercept particles. TURBOCORE™ intercepts the full contamination spectrum — particulate, free water, emulsified water and dissolved water — through a sequenced three-stage protection architecture that operates independently of fuel flow rate and back-pressure conditions.

The 900FH and 1000FH models deploy HYDROCORE™ turbine rotation as the first line of defense — a passive inertial system that requires no electronic control, no actuators and no maintenance intervention. Protection begins the moment fuel enters the housing.

The result: a fuel injection circuit that receives verified, clean fuel — every cycle, every hour, regardless of source fuel quality or operating conditions.

TURBOCORE™ · 900FH / 1000FH

EACH STAGE ELIMINATES WHAT THE PREVIOUS ONE CANNOT.

HYDROCORE™ turbine rotation induces centrifugal momentum on incoming fuel, driving macro-particles and free water outward against the chamber wall by inertial force alone. No moving parts. Zero mechanical dependency. The intercepted mass accumulates in the sealed base chamber — permanently separated from the fuel path before the protection sequence even begins.

WHERE TURBOCORE™ PROTECTS

FH-series turbine protection systems are validated for land-based combustion asset protection. Marine applications are served by the FM series.

HEAVY TRANSPORT

Long-haul diesel drawn from shared depot storage tanks accumulates water contamination from condensation cycles in the supply chain. At 1,800+ bar injection pressure, a free-water event in the HPCR rail can hydraulically fracture an injector — an event that takes the vehicle out of service for days, not hours. Water content in depot-sourced diesel is rarely tested before delivery.

POWER GENERATION

Diesel generators on standby service sit with full fuel tanks for weeks between test cycles. Condensation during this storage creates progressive water accumulation at the tank bottom. When the genset starts under emergency load, the first fuel drawn arrives at the injection system already carrying the accumulated contamination from the storage period.

AGRICULTURE

On-farm diesel storage in above-ground tanks undergoes extreme diurnal temperature swings through transition seasons. Each cycle deposits condensation on tank walls that runs to the draw point. Combined with breather ingress during rain events, seasonal water accumulation in farm tanks is a predictable rather than exceptional contamination source.

MINING

Bulk fuel deliveries to remote mining sites travel long distances over rough roads. Transport vibration re-emulsifies any settled water back into suspension, delivering it in the exact condition most damaging to injection tolerances — fine droplets uniformly dispersed through the fuel and invisible to inspection.

CONSTRUCTION

Mobile refueling bowsers on active construction sites are exposed to rain, dust and humidity during every fueling operation. Water enters through loose filler caps, during outdoor drum transfers and through tank condensation. Contaminated refueling is the leading source of injection system water ingress in mobile construction equipment.

RAILWAY

Diesel locomotives are fueled at service depots, remote sidings and maintenance yards with inconsistent fuel quality standards. Water contamination that accumulates across multiple refueling events reaches the injection system gradually — causing progressive erosion rather than a single detectable contamination event.
