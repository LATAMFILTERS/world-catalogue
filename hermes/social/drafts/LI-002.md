## Draft copy

Modern diesel fuel systems changed what “clean fuel” means.

High-pressure common rail systems operate with extremely tight internal clearances. At that scale, contamination that would have been tolerated by older injection systems can become a direct wear mechanism for injectors, control valves and high-pressure pumps.

That is why fuel cleanliness is not only about removing visible dirt.

It is about controlling particles, water and fuel-condition factors before they reach precision components.

Three engineering questions matter:

• How clean is the fuel entering the system?
• How effectively is the filter removing damaging particles?
• Is water being controlled before it reaches the high-pressure side?

Standards such as ISO 23369, ISO 12156-1 and ASTM D6304 provide a framework for evaluating filtration performance, lubricity and water content in modern diesel fuel systems.

The filtration strategy has to match the sensitivity of the system it is protecting.

## Evidence boundary

Approved concepts used in this draft:
- HPCR systems are materially more contamination-sensitive than legacy diesel injection systems.
- HPCR protection requires control of particulate contamination, water and fuel condition.
- ISO 23369 addresses fuel-filter multi-pass performance for injection-engine liquid fuel.
- ISO 12156-1 addresses diesel fuel lubricity by HFRR.
- ASTM D6304 is used to quantify water in petroleum products by Karl Fischer titration.
