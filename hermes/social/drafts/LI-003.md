## Published copy

Clear fuel is not necessarily dry fuel.

In diesel systems, water contamination is not always obvious. Fuel may look clean to the eye and still contain water levels that can affect system reliability, component protection and combustion performance.

That is why fuel cleanliness is not only about particles.

It is also about controlling water before it reaches sensitive components.

Water in fuel can contribute to corrosion, reduce lubricity, interfere with injector performance and create conditions that compromise the protection of the fuel system.

The practical question is not simply whether fuel looks clean.

It is whether the fuel entering the system is being protected against both particulate contamination and water-related risk.

Effective fuel-water separation becomes an important part of protecting modern diesel equipment, especially in demanding operating environments where storage, handling and exposure conditions can make contamination harder to control.
