## 1. Distributor Acquisition

### 1.1 Target Distributor Profile

The highest-value ELIMFILTERS distributor is not the largest equipment parts dealer in a region. It is the dealer who already sells filtration but lacks a filtration brand with technical depth. This profile is widespread in LATAM industrial markets:

- **Current state**: Stocks Donaldson, Fleetguard, or Mann as stock items; sells on price and part-number cross-reference; has no technical authority with fleet maintenance managers
- **Pain point**: Losing margin to OEM dealers who can justify a specification conversation; losing accounts when buyers go direct to brand websites
- **What ELIMFILTERS provides**: A filtration brand they can own the technical narrative on; cross-reference tools; knowledge system content they can use in customer conversations; technical training they can deliver

Target distributor size: 5–50 employees, USD $2M–$20M annual parts revenue, at least one dedicated industrial/mining/agricultural account.

### 1.2 Distributor Recruitment Funnel

**Stage 1 — Identification**
Build a longlist of distributors in each target market through: trade association membership directories (LATAM equipment dealers associations), OEM dealer networks (Caterpillar, Komatsu, John Deere authorised service centres), LinkedIn company search by category, and referrals from existing contacts.

Target 50 qualified prospects per priority market before outreach begins.

**Stage 2 — Authority-First Outreach**
Do not lead with product or price. Lead with the knowledge system. First contact delivers a specific technical insight relevant to their primary market — a one-page summary from the Knowledge System on particle wear in mining haul trucks, or water contamination in agricultural fuel systems. The implicit message: ELIMFILTERS knows their customer's problem better than their current supplier does.

Outreach sequence:
- Day 1: Technical insight document (email, no CTA)
- Day 7: Follow-up with cross-reference capability demonstration for their top OEM accounts
- Day 14: Distributor economics conversation if engaged

**Stage 3 — Onboarding**
Distributors who commit receive: exclusive territory agreement (where applicable), initial stock package with recommended inventory by industry mix, technical training programme (2-day, delivered at their site), co-branded marketing materials, access to Part Search for their sales team, and a named account contact at ELIMFILTERS.

**Stage 4 — Activation**
A distributor who has not placed a reorder within 60 days of initial stock delivery is not activated. Assign a commercial manager to each new distributor with a 90-day activation plan: 3 joint customer calls, 1 fleet presentation, 1 technical training delivered to their key customer.

### 1.3 Distributor Economics Positioning

The conversation that converts a distributor is not about margin percentage. It is about account retention. Position it as:

*"Your current filter supplier gives you a product. ELIMFILTERS gives you a technical story. A Donaldson cross-reference gives your customer a reason to go direct to Donaldson. An ELIMFILTERS specification conversation gives them a reason to stay with you."*

Target gross margin for distributors: 28–35% depending on volume tier and exclusivity. This is competitive with Donaldson and above Fleetguard OEM pricing for similar product classes.

## 3. CANIDRA Strategy

### 3.1 Framework Overview

CANIDRA is the structured distributor development model for ELIMFILTERS channel expansion. It defines five stages that take a prospect from first contact to a revenue-generating, technically capable distribution partner:

**C — Capture** (Market intelligence and prospect identification)
Map the distributor landscape in each territory before any outreach. Capture includes: company profiling, current supplier identification, primary industry exposure, estimated annual filtration spend, and key decision-maker identification. No outreach without a complete capture profile.

**A — Authority Transfer** (Technical positioning with the prospect)
The distributor must believe ELIMFILTERS knows more about their customer's contamination problems than their current supplier does. Authority transfer is achieved through: a customised industry insight document delivered before any product conversation, a live Part Search demonstration showing cross-reference depth, and a technical comparison of ELIMFILTERS media specification vs their current stocked brand.

**N — Network Activation** (Joint customer development)
The first three months of a new distributor relationship are defined by joint selling, not independent selling. ELIMFILTERS commercial and technical staff participate in customer calls alongside the distributor's sales team. The goal is to transfer the authority positioning from ELIMFILTERS to the distributor — so their salespeople can run the technical conversation independently within 90 days.

**I — Infrastructure** (Inventory, documentation, and tools)
A distributor cannot sell a product their customers cannot find in the system. Infrastructure stage ensures: correct inventory depth by industry mix, Part Search access and training for all sales staff, co-branded technical documentation available in the local language, and OEM cross-reference database access.

**D — Data** (Performance measurement and account development)
Measure distributor performance at 30/60/90/180 days: first reorder date, revenue ramp rate, account penetration (number of unique customers), and technical capability (can their team run a contamination conversation without ELIMFILTERS support). Data drives the decision to invest more in a distributor, reassign territory, or terminate.

**R — Retention** (Ongoing support and loyalty programme)
Distributors who have been active for 12+ months enter the retention programme: annual joint business planning, volume rebate structure, technical training refresh, co-funded marketing activities, and early access to new product introductions. The retention programme is what prevents a successful distributor from being poached by Donaldson or Mann.

**A — Amplification** (Reference accounts and case studies)
A distributor with a demonstrable customer success story (documented contamination problem solved, equipment life extended, downtime reduced) becomes a reference asset. Amplification converts individual account wins into market-level authority: case study published on the Knowledge System, distributor featured in trade media, customer willing to present at industry events.

## 4. Fleet Strategy

### 4.1 Fleet Opportunity Definition

A fleet account is any operator of 20+ vehicles or machines using the same engine platform across their fleet. Fleet accounts are worth 5–10× individual distributor account value because:
- Standardised specification across the fleet means one winning conversation covers all machines
- Service interval schedules create predictable recurring order patterns
- Fleet managers have authority to specify suppliers — they are not price-shopping individual filters

Target: logistics fleets (long-haul trucks, delivery), municipal fleets (refuse, utility, transit buses), mining contractor fleets (on-mine service vehicles), and agricultural co-operative machinery pools.

### 4.2 Fleet Sales Process

**Step 1 — Fleet Audit**
The entry point for a fleet account conversation is not a product pitch. It is a free fleet filtration audit: ELIMFILTERS technical staff review the fleet's current filtration specification, identify gaps (wrong efficiency rating, incorrect service intervals, missing domain coverage), and deliver a written audit report with quantified risk assessment.

The audit report is the sales document. It does not mention ELIMFILTERS products until section 4 of 5. Sections 1–3 establish the problem. Section 4 presents the solution. Section 5 is the commercial proposal.

**Step 2 — Pilot Programme**
Never ask a fleet manager to change their entire fleet specification at once. Propose a 90-day pilot on one equipment type or one site. Measurable outcomes: oil analysis results, filter service intervals, any downtime incidents.

Pilot success criteria are agreed in writing before the pilot begins. This prevents post-pilot negotiation and creates a clear handover to full fleet conversion.

**Step 3 — Full Fleet Conversion**
After a successful pilot, the conversion conversation is led by the pilot data, not by ELIMFILTERS sales materials. The fleet manager's own maintenance records show the outcome. The commercial conversation is TCO-based: annual filter cost across the fleet, projected downtime reduction, service interval extension value.

**Step 4 — Fleet Account Management**
A converted fleet account has a named ELIMFILTERS account manager who reviews specification annually, flags new machine additions that require filter changes, and provides oil analysis interpretation support. Fleet accounts that receive proactive technical support have 95%+ retention rates.

### 4.3 Fleet Pricing Architecture

Fleet accounts receive volume pricing with a transparent tier structure published to distributors. Three tiers:
- **Fleet Tier 1**: 20–99 vehicles — 8% below standard distributor price
- **Fleet Tier 2**: 100–499 vehicles — 15% below standard distributor price
- **Fleet Tier 3**: 500+ vehicles — negotiated annual contract with rebate structure

Fleet pricing flows through the distributor, not direct. The fleet account is managed by ELIMFILTERS; the supply relationship runs through the distributor. This protects distributor margin while allowing ELIMFILTERS to control the specification conversation.

## 5. Mining Strategy

### 5.1 Market Characteristics

Mining is ELIMFILTERS' highest-value single-industry opportunity. The contamination challenge — extreme silica and hardrock dust at 5,000–15,000 mg/m³ — is the most demanding air intake environment in any industry. MACROCORE™ at 99.9%–99.98% efficiency is a genuine performance story, not a marketing claim. Machine downtime cost at $180,000/hour makes filter specification a C-suite decision, not a procurement decision.

The challenge: mining specification changes are slow and risk-averse. A maintenance manager whose engine failed after a filter change will not change brands again for years. Credibility and trust are built over multiple contacts before any specification change is considered.

### 5.2 Mining Entry Points

**Open-pit gold and copper mining** — Highest equipment density, highest dust exposure, highest downtime cost. Entry via: mine maintenance superintendents at operations planning stage (12 months before scheduled filter brand review), not during active maintenance season.

**Quarry and aggregate operations** — Smaller operations, shorter sales cycles, lower switching risk for the buyer. Quarry operations are the mining beachhead — easier to win, and the case study from a quarry operation creates credibility for the conversation at a larger mine.

**Mining contractors** — Companies that operate equipment on behalf of mine owners (drill and blast, load and haul, mine development) often have more purchasing flexibility than major mining companies. They also operate the same equipment classes across multiple sites, multiplying the value of a single specification win.

### 5.3 Mining Specification Strategy

Mining companies specify filtration by OEM recommendation or internal maintenance standard. To change a specification, ELIMFILTERS must:

1. **Get on the approved supplier list** — submit product documentation, ISO 5011 test certificates, and cross-reference data to the mine's procurement system. This is administrative, not commercial. Do it first.

2. **Build the maintenance manager relationship** — the specification decision is made by the maintenance superintendent, not procurement. The relationship is built through technical conversations, not product presentations. Use the Knowledge System dust ingestion documentation as the basis for the first conversation.

3. **Propose a controlled trial** — identify one equipment type for a 6-month trial with agreed measurement criteria: oil analysis results (silicon content as proxy for dust ingestion), filter service life, any engine incidents. Controlled trial removes the risk perception for the maintenance manager.

4. **Document and publish the outcome** — a mining case study with named mine, measured outcomes, and equipment types is worth more than any advertising budget for the next 10 accounts.

### 5.4 Mining Account Value Model

| Equipment class | Filter types | Annual spend per machine | Fleet size | Annual contract value |
|-----------------|-------------|-------------------------|------------|----------------------|
| 400-tonne haul truck | Air ×4, Oil ×2, Fuel ×2, Hydraulic ×4 | USD $8,000–$15,000 | 20–80 machines | USD $160,000–$1,200,000 |
| Drill rig | Air ×6, Oil ×2, Fuel ×2 | USD $5,000–$10,000 | 5–20 rigs | USD $25,000–$200,000 |
| Loader/excavator | Air ×2, Oil ×1, Fuel ×1, Hydraulic ×2 | USD $2,000–$4,000 | 10–30 machines | USD $20,000–$120,000 |

A single mid-sized open-pit operation (30 haul trucks, 8 drill rigs, 15 loaders) represents USD $500,000–$2,000,000 annual filtration spend. Winning 5 accounts of this type is a $5M–$10M revenue milestone.

## 6. Construction Strategy

### 6.1 Market Characteristics

Construction differs from mining in three important ways: higher equipment mobility (machines move between sites), shorter project cycles (a contract ends, equipment is redeployed or sold), and more fragmented ownership (large contractors but also many owner-operators with 5–20 machines). The highest-value construction accounts are large civil contractors and infrastructure project operators with fleets of 50+ machines deployed on long-duration projects (18 months+).

### 6.2 Construction Entry Points

**Large civil contractors** — Companies executing road, dam, port, and urban infrastructure projects. These contractors have centralised purchasing and maintenance departments. The entry is through the equipment manager or plant manager, not site supervisors.

**Equipment rental companies** — Rental fleets operate diverse equipment across multiple customers and care intensely about maintenance cost and uptime. A rental company that standardises on ELIMFILTERS specification across their fleet is both a direct revenue account and a distribution channel — when they sell or decommission machines, those machines go to market with ELIMFILTERS cross-reference history.

**OEM dealer service departments** — Caterpillar, Komatsu, Volvo, and Liebherr dealers operate their own maintenance workshops and stock filtration products for in-house service work. Positioning ELIMFILTERS as the dealer's filtration brand (rather than OEM filter parts) requires demonstrating the price/performance case and providing cross-reference depth that covers their entire fleet service exposure.

### 6.3 Construction Multi-Domain Pitch

The construction pitch is the strongest multi-domain argument in the ELIMFILTERS portfolio. A construction fleet needs:
- Air filtration (MACROCORE) for engine protection in silica dust
- Hydraulic filtration (NANOFORCE) for proportional valve protection in excavators and loaders
- Cabin air (MICROKAPPA) for operator health (PM10 compliance) on sites near demolition or aggregate crushing
- Fuel filtration (HYDROCORE) for machines using stored fuel in remote locations
- Lube oil (SYNTRAX) for engine bearing protection across the fleet

This is a USD $4,000–$8,000 per machine per year multi-domain opportunity. Lead with air — it is the easiest technical conversation — then expand to hydraulic and cabin once the initial relationship is established.

### 6.4 Construction Selling Calendar

Construction purchasing decisions follow project mobilisation cycles. The best time to approach a contractor is 3–6 months before a major project mobilisation — when they are planning equipment deployment and setting up supply agreements for the project duration.

Track major infrastructure project awards in target markets (public tender databases, engineering news sources) and map them to target contractor companies. A contractor who just won a 3-year infrastructure contract worth $500M is in active procurement mode for all equipment consumables.

## 7. LATAM Expansion

### 7.1 Market Prioritisation

LATAM offers significant opportunity across mining, agriculture, construction, and commercial transport. Not all LATAM markets are equivalent. Prioritise by: industrial activity density, market accessibility (logistics, currency risk, regulatory environment), and existing ELIMFILTERS footprint.

**Tier 1 — Immediate priority** (H2 2026):
- **Chile** — World-class copper mining (Escondida, Collahuasi, Spence), sophisticated procurement, USD-denominated contracts, stable regulatory environment. Highest single-market value opportunity.
- **Brazil** — Largest industrial economy in LATAM. Massive agriculture (soy, corn, sugarcane harvesting) and mining (Vale iron ore, gold). Complex tax environment but enormous scale.
- **Peru** — Major gold and copper mining (Las Bambas, Cerro Verde, Yanacocha), growing construction sector.

**Tier 2 — Medium-term** (H1 2027):
- **Colombia** — Growing mining and construction; oil and gas presence
- **Mexico** — Large manufacturing and automotive fleet market; significant agriculture
- **Argentina** — Agricultural dominance (Pampas soy/corn), recovery-dependent timing

**Tier 3 — Opportunistic**:
- Ecuador, Bolivia, Paraguay — smaller markets, serve via Tier 1/2 distributors where geography permits

### 7.2 LATAM Distribution Model

LATAM requires a two-tier distribution model in most markets:

**Tier 1 (Regional Importer/Master Distributor)**: One company per country holds import rights, manages customs/regulatory compliance, warehouses central inventory, and supplies the Tier 2 network. This company is typically an established industrial parts importer, not a filter specialist. They bring logistics and compliance; ELIMFILTERS brings brand and specification authority.

**Tier 2 (Regional Dealers)**: Specialist equipment dealers, agricultural input distributors, and mining supply companies who have direct relationships with end customers. They buy from the Tier 1 importer and sell with technical support from ELIMFILTERS.

This model reduces ELIMFILTERS' operational complexity while ensuring last-mile coverage in remote mining and agricultural regions.

### 7.3 LATAM Localisation Requirements

**Language**: All technical documentation, cross-reference tools, and training materials must be in Portuguese (Brazil) and Spanish (all other markets). The Knowledge System Spanish content is an asset; ensure it is technically accurate for LATAM equipment terminology (South American Spanish mining vocabulary differs from Iberian).

**Currency and payment**: LATAM distributors operate in local currencies with inflation exposure. Price ELIMFILTERS products in USD but support payment in local currency with transparent exchange rate policies. Offer 60–90 day payment terms for established distributors (standard for the region).

**Regulatory**: Verify import classification and tariff treatment in each Tier 1 market before appointing a distributor. Some LATAM markets (Brazil in particular) have complex import regulations for industrial consumables that require specific product classification and documentation.

**Trade events**: LATAM industrial trade events are the primary channel for initial distributor identification and relationship building. Key events: Exponor (Chile, mining), Agraria (Peru, agriculture), Exposibram (Brazil, mining), Agroshow (Brazil, agriculture), ExpoConstruccion (Colombia, construction). Budget for 4–6 events per year across Tier 1 markets.

### 7.4 LATAM Knowledge System Localisation

The existing 11-language Knowledge System is a structural advantage in LATAM markets where competitors have no equivalent technical content in Spanish or Portuguese. Use this actively:

- Ensure Knowledge System Spanish and Portuguese translations are technically reviewed by native-speaker engineers (not just translation tools)
- Create LATAM-specific case studies featuring regional equipment (Komatsu 930E haul trucks used in Chilean copper mining; Massey Ferguson combiners used in Brazilian soy harvest)
- Register elimfilters.com domain presence in .cl, .br, .pe country extensions and redirect to the main Knowledge System with localised content

## 8. Lead Generation

### 8.1 Inbound Lead Architecture

The Knowledge System functions as the primary inbound lead generation asset. Technical buyers — maintenance managers, fleet superintendents, procurement engineers — search for specific contamination problems and standards. The Knowledge System is already structured to capture these queries.

Inbound lead conversion requires three additions to the existing Knowledge System:

**Gated depth content**: Behind a name/email/company form, offer downloadable technical documents that have no equivalent in public search results:
- Equipment-specific filter selection guides (e.g., "Komatsu 930E Complete Filtration Specification")
- Industry contamination benchmarking reports (e.g., "Mining Haul Truck Filtration Performance Benchmark 2026")

**Part Search lead capture**: When a user runs a Part Search that finds matches, prompt with: "Save this specification for your fleet — create a free account." A saved search becomes a qualified lead.

**Technical consultation CTA**: On every Knowledge System domain page, a consistent CTA: "Talk to an ELIMFILTERS engineer about your contamination challenge." This routes to a short intake form (industry, equipment type, current filter brand, primary concern). Routed to a technical sales engineer within 24 hours.

### 8.2 Outbound Lead Generation

**LinkedIn outreach** — Target maintenance engineers, plant managers, fleet managers, and procurement managers at mining, construction, and agricultural companies in target markets. Personalised outreach leading with a specific insight about their industry's contamination challenge. Sequence: connection request with personalised note → insight share (no pitch) → technical document → conversation invitation.

**Trade association engagement** — Join and become visible in: LATAM mining associations (SONAMI Chile, IBRAM Brazil), agricultural machinery associations, fleet management associations. ### 8.3 Lead Qualification Criteria

Not all leads are equal. Score leads on:
- **Industry fit**: Mining/Construction/Agriculture = HIGH; General transport = MEDIUM; Unknown = LOW
- **Equipment volume**: 20+ machines = HIGH; 5–19 = MEDIUM; <5 = LOW

Leads scoring HIGH on 3+ criteria go immediately to technical sales. Leads scoring HIGH on 1–2 criteria go into a nurture sequence (monthly technical content, quarterly personal contact).

No competitor in ELIMFILTERS' addressable market has published a structured, machine-readable industrial knowledge system. Donaldson, Fleetguard, Mann, and Baldwin publish product catalogues and cross-reference databases. None publish a systematic contamination → technology → protection framework with ISO standard integration.

This gap is the authority opportunity. Authority building is the process of making this advantage visible to the buyers who will value it.

### 9.2 Content Authority Programme

**Technical article series**: Publish one long-form technical article per month in the Knowledge System. Topics drawn from the vault's Article entity type (defined in Phase 3A but not yet created): contamination diagnosis case studies, equipment-specific filter selection methodology, industry benchmark reports. Each article references the canonical knowledge entities in the vault and links to the relevant Knowledge System domain pages.

They send oil analysis results; ELIMFILTERS provides a written interpretation with contamination source identification and filter specification recommendation. Cost: 30 minutes of engineer time per sample. Value: demonstrates contamination knowledge depth that no competitor offers for free, and creates a direct conversation channel with the maintenance manager.

**Industry standards commentary**: When ISO standards are revised (ISO 5011, ISO 16889, ISO 4406), publish a technical commentary on what changed and what it means for equipment maintenance programmes. Technical buyers who find ELIMFILTERS' standards commentary via search build a strong association between the brand and technical authority.

**Distributor certification programme**: Create an ELIMFILTERS Technical Certification for distributor sales staff — a structured online course covering contamination modes, filter selection methodology, and oil analysis interpretation. Certified staff receive a credential they can display in customer conversations. This serves three purposes: it improves distributor technical capability, it builds distributor loyalty (they have invested in the certification), and it creates an authority signal visible to end customers.

### 9.4 Trade Media Programme

Identify 3–5 trade publications in each priority market that reach maintenance engineers and fleet managers. Targets: Mining Magazine, Machinery Trader, Agricultural Machinery Journal equivalents in each LATAM market. Offer:

## 10. Conversion Strategy

### 10.1 Conversion Architecture

The ELIMFILTERS conversion model has three closing mechanisms, matched to buyer type:

**Technical buyer close** (maintenance managers, fleet superintendents): Closes on specification logic — the contamination → standard → technology → product chain. The close is: "This is the specification that solves your problem. Here is the data that proves it." The Knowledge System and the oil analysis programme are the primary tools.

**Commercial buyer close** (procurement managers, finance teams): Closes on TCO — total annual cost of filtration across the fleet, downtime cost avoided, service interval extension value. The close is: "Here is what you spend today. Here is what you spend with ELIMFILTERS. Here is the maintenance event you avoid." Always expressed in the buyer's local currency with their specific equipment fleet.

**Risk buyer close** (operations directors, mine managers): Closes on risk elimination — what happens if filtration fails, how much does one engine failure cost, what is the probability reduction from specifying correctly. The close is: "The risk of a $2M engine failure from a $200 specification decision is not a position any operations manager can defend." Never said aggressively; said as a shared problem statement.

### 10.2 Proposal Framework

Every ELIMFILTERS commercial proposal follows a five-section structure regardless of market or buyer type:

1. **The Problem** — Contamination challenge specific to their industry and equipment. Uses their language, their equipment types, their operating conditions. No ELIMFILTERS branding in this section.

2. **The Risk** — Quantified consequence of uncontrolled contamination. Specific to their fleet size. Downtime cost, component replacement cost, maintenance interval compression.

3. **The Standard** — What ISO/SAE/ASTM standards specify for their application. Positions the specification conversation on objective measurement, not brand preference.

4. **The Solution** — ELIMFILTERS products matched to the contamination challenge. ISO certifications cited. Performance data from test reports, not marketing claims.

5. **The Commercial** — Pricing, volume tiers, service terms. Comes last because it is the least important part of the conversation until trust is established by sections 1–4.

### 10.3 Objection Handling

**"We already use Donaldson/Fleetguard."**
Response: "Good choice — they're reliable products. Our conversation isn't about replacing them with something equivalent. It's about whether your current specification addresses the contamination profile of your specific equipment operating in your specific environment. Can I show you what we've found in similar operations?"

**"Your price is higher."**
Response: "Per filter, possibly. Per engine overhaul avoided, definitely not. Can we look at your fleet's annual filter spend as a percentage of your unplanned maintenance cost? That's the calculation that tells you whether filter price is the right number to optimise."

**"We don't change suppliers without a trial."**
Response: "Agreed — that's the right approach. Let's define what a 90-day trial looks like for your highest-value equipment type, agree the measurement criteria upfront, and let the results make the decision."

**"We need OEM-approved products."**
Response: "Understood. Our products meet the same ISO standards as OEM filtration. OEM approval is a specification question, not a quality question. Let's look at the OEM specification for your equipment and confirm our products meet it — in most cases they exceed it."

**"We've never heard of ELIMFILTERS."**
Response: "That's fair. Let me leave you with our technical documentation on contamination control for your industry. It's not a sales document — it's the same knowledge we use to train our engineers. If it's useful, we can have a technical conversation. If it's not, no pressure."

### 10.4 Contract Structure

**Mining and large fleet contracts**: Annual supply agreements with:
- Agreed product list (specific SKUs by equipment type)
- Volume commitment with quarterly review
- Pricing fixed for 12 months with CPI adjustment clause
- Technical support hours included (quarterly site visit, oil analysis review)
- Stock guarantee (ELIMFILTERS commits to supply continuity)

**Distributor agreements**: Three-year framework with:
- Exclusive territory (specific geography, specific industry verticals)
- Annual minimum purchase commitment (USD-denominated)
- Training and certification requirements
- Co-marketing fund (2% of annual purchases, allocated to joint activities)
- Performance review at 6/12/24/36 months

**Fleet accounts**: 2-year supply agreements with:
- Full fleet specification (all machine types, all filter domains)
- Annual volume forecast with 20% flexibility
- Price stability for 24 months
- Regular technical review meetings included
