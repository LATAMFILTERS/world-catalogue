**Purpose:** Resolve the three internal decisions that prevent distributor recruitment from starting

## CONTEXT

The distributor recruitment plan (DISTRIBUTOR_RECRUITMENT_LAUNCH_PLAN.md) identified three blocking decisions that must be made internally before any prospect is contacted. These are not presentation decisions or marketing decisions — they are foundational commercial and legal decisions that determine what ELIMFILTERS® can commit to in a distributor agreement.

An outreach campaign that generates discovery calls but cannot answer "who do I sign a contract with?", "how long until I receive my first order?", or "what margin will I earn?" will generate qualified interest that converts to signed competitors.

**The three blockers:**

1. **Legal contracting entity** — Which legal entity signs the distributor agreement, and how is the Kleo Technologies / ELIMFILTERS® relationship explained to candidates doing due diligence?

2. **Preliminary supply chain lead-time model** — What is the estimated transit time and logistics route from the ELIMFILTERS® supply point to each Tier 1 distributor market? What are the implications of the Venezuela LATAM operations base?

3. **Distributor margin architecture** — What wholesale margin does a new distributor earn, and how does that margin change with volume?

Each decision is presented below with available options, their advantages and risks, and a specific recommendation.

# BLOCKER 1 — LEGAL CONTRACTING ENTITY

## 1.1 Decision Required

**Which legal entity signs the ELIMFILTERS® authorized distributor agreement, and how is the relationship between that entity and Kleo Technologies communicated to prospective distributors?**

This decision affects: every distributor agreement, every commercial communication, the governing law and dispute resolution mechanism, and the due diligence a distributor will conduct before committing.

## 1.2 Current State

The brand operates under the name ELIMFILTERS®. The website footer states "© 2015–2026 ELIMFILTERS® — Kleo Technologies" (updated per Phase 1 implementation). The About page now states: "ELIMFILTERS® is an advanced filtration solutions brand built on proprietary technologies owned and developed by Kleo Technologies."

The following is unknown from publicly available information:
- Whether ELIMFILTERS® LLC is a registered legal entity or only a brand name
- Whether contracts are currently signed under Kleo Technologies as the parent entity
- The state/country of registration of each entity
- The operational relationship between the Texas HQ (Frisco, Texas) and the Venezuela LATAM operations (Barquisimeto)

A distributor doing basic due diligence — which every serious industrial distributor will do before signing an agreement — will search both names, look for company registrations, and form a judgment about entity legitimacy. If the registration search produces no results for "ELIMFILTERS® LLC" but finds "Kleo Technologies" with a different commercial identity, this creates friction or abandonment.

## 1.3 Available Options

---

### Option A — Contract as Kleo Technologies (Parent Company)

The distributor agreement is signed between Kleo Technologies and the distributor. ELIMFILTERS® is identified in the agreement as the brand name under which Kleo Technologies operates the filtration product line.

**Advantages:**
- Uses the registered legal entity — no new formation required
- Due diligence on Kleo Technologies returns a real registered company with history
- Standard structure for brands operated by a parent holding company
- Legally clean: one entity, one set of representations and warranties

**Risks:**
- Distributors searching "Kleo Technologies" find a different brand identity, which may create confusion about the brand they are actually representing
- If Kleo Technologies operates other business lines, a distributor's legal counsel may raise questions about cross-liability
- The distributor agreement must explicitly define what is and is not included in the ELIMFILTERS® program scope — if Kleo Technologies has other product lines, the distributor needs clarity on what they are and are not authorized to sell

**Appropriate when:**
- ELIMFILTERS® LLC does not exist as a registered entity and company leadership does not want to form it before recruitment begins
- Kleo Technologies is a well-established entity with a clean registration history
- The brand relationship (Kleo Technologies = parent, ELIMFILTERS® = brand) is clearly stated in writing

---

### Option B — Contract as ELIMFILTERS® LLC (Brand Entity)

A legal entity called "ELIMFILTERS® LLC" is registered (if not already) and acts as the sole contracting party for all distributor agreements. Kleo Technologies may be referenced in the agreement as the parent or sole member of the LLC.

**Advantages:**
- Creates complete separation between the distributor-facing brand and the parent company — no due diligence confusion
- All distributor agreements, warranties, and commercial commitments are isolated within the ELIMFILTERS® LLC entity
- Distributors search "ELIMFILTERS® LLC" and find a registered company that matches the brand they are evaluating
- Scales cleanly: as the distributor network grows, all commercial relationships are consolidated within one entity

**Risks:**
- If ELIMFILTERS® LLC is not yet registered, formation takes 1–3 weeks and incurs legal costs ($500–$2,000 USD depending on state and whether counsel is used)
- A newly registered LLC (if registered recently) will return minimal history in due diligence searches — distributors may ask "how old is this entity?"
- Intercompany agreements between ELIMFILTERS® LLC and Kleo Technologies must be in place to govern product supply, IP licensing, and financial flows

**Appropriate when:**
- ELIMFILTERS® LLC already exists or leadership is willing to form it before recruitment begins
- Company counsel can draft the necessary intercompany agreement between the two entities within 2–3 weeks

---

### Option C — Kleo Technologies DBA ELIMFILTERS®

The agreement is signed by "Kleo Technologies, doing business as ELIMFILTERS®" — a DBA (fictitious business name) registration that formally links the two names under one entity.

**Advantages:**
- No new entity formation required — faster and less expensive than Option B
- Explicitly links both names in a single legally recognized identity
- Distributor search results for either name lead back to the same registered entity
- Common structure for branded product lines operated by an established parent

**Risks:**
- DBA registration is state-specific and must be completed in the state where Kleo Technologies is registered (assumed Texas based on Frisco HQ)
- Does not provide liability isolation between the two brands — Kleo Technologies' other activities remain part of the same legal risk profile as ELIMFILTERS®
- Some international distributors or their legal counsel may be unfamiliar with the DBA structure and may request a standalone entity for the agreement

**Appropriate when:**
- Company wants to move fast and avoid entity formation
- Kleo Technologies does not have other business lines that could create contract interpretation confusion

---

### Option D — Two-Tier Structure: Kleo Technologies Signs, ELIMFILTERS® Program Manages

The distributor agreement is signed by Kleo Technologies. Day-to-day program management (purchase orders, support, communications) operates under the ELIMFILTERS® brand. The agreement explicitly names the ELIMFILTERS® Authorized Distributor Program as the program the distributor is joining.

**Advantages:**
- No new entity formation
- Aligns with how many brand programs operate in practice — legal entity is the parent, brand is the operational identity
- Lowest administrative cost and fastest to implement

**Risks:**
- Requires careful agreement language to make clear that ELIMFILTERS® program commitments are backed by Kleo Technologies' full legal authority
- Confusion risk is highest here: distributor signs with Kleo Technologies but receives all program communications from "ELIMFILTERS® Commercial Team" — they may question why the names differ
- If Kleo Technologies' other business creates any commercial or reputational issue, it directly touches the ELIMFILTERS® distributor relationship

---

## 1.4 Recommended Approach

**Recommendation: Option B — ELIMFILTERS® LLC as the contracting entity.**

The brand positioning investment is significant. The Knowledge System, the Part Search tool, the distributor program, and all associated marketing materials carry the ELIMFILTERS® identity. A distributor who commits to the program is committing to ELIMFILTERS® — not to Kleo Technologies. The legal structure should match that reality.

If ELIMFILTERS® LLC is not yet registered: file in Texas (or the state of Kleo Technologies' registration) immediately. Texas LLC formation can be completed in under one week through the Secretary of State's online portal. Cost: approximately $300 USD in state fees. Basic intercompany services agreement between Kleo Technologies and ELIMFILTERS® LLC should be drafted concurrently.

**If forming ELIMFILTERS® LLC before outreach is not feasible:** use Option C (Kleo Technologies DBA ELIMFILTERS®) as the interim solution. File the DBA, complete all communications under the DBA name, and convert to the LLC structure before the first distributor agreement is signed.

**Do not use Option A without at minimum filing Option C.** An agreement signed as "Kleo Technologies" without any registered link to the ELIMFILTERS® name creates a due diligence gap that will surface in every serious candidate evaluation.

## 1.5 Communication Template for Candidates

Regardless of which option is chosen, the relationship must be explained proactively in the discovery call — not left for candidates to discover. The following one-paragraph explanation can be used verbally in the discovery call and in the program one-pager:

> "ELIMFILTERS® is a filtration solutions brand operated by Kleo Technologies, headquartered in Frisco, Texas. The distributor agreement you will sign is with [chosen entity]. Kleo Technologies owns the product line, the intellectual property, and the manufacturing relationships. ELIMFILTERS® is the brand under which the authorized distributor program operates."

# BLOCKER 2 — SUPPLY CHAIN LEAD-TIME MODEL

## 2.1 Decision Required

**What is the logistics route and estimated transit time from the ELIMFILTERS® supply point to authorized distributors in Colombia, Chile, and Mexico? Does the supply chain run through Venezuela, and if so, what are the implications?**

This decision affects: the credibility of the 30-day inventory coverage requirement, the distributor's cash planning (they need to know when to reorder), the program opening order payment structure, and the due diligence questions about Venezuela that will arise in every discovery call.

## 2.2 Current State

The DISTRIBUTOR_PROGRAM_DECISION_WORKBOOK.md defines a 30-day rolling inventory coverage requirement. This requirement is only credible if ELIMFILTERS® can replenish inventory in under 30 days from the distributor's reorder point.

The COMMERCIAL_CONVERSION_AUDIT.md identifies two geographic facts that are publicly visible on the website:
- **US HQ:** Frisco, Texas
- **LATAM Operations:** Barquisimeto, Venezuela

Both of these locations are relevant to the supply chain. The audit flags Venezuela's LATAM base as a potential logistics concern for distributors in Colombia, Chile, and Mexico, citing: currency risk, sanctions compliance, and logistics reliability.

No current documentation confirms:
- Where finished products are manufactured or warehoused
- Whether LATAM shipments originate from Texas, Venezuela, or a third location
- Current transit times to any Tier 1 market
- Whether any OFAC or trade compliance considerations affect Venezuelan-origin or Venezuelan-transited shipments

## 2.3 The Venezuela Question

This must be addressed directly before any discovery call, because it will be raised. There are three possible realities, each requiring a different response:

**Reality A — Products ship from the US (Texas). Venezuela is a commercial operations center only.**

If inventory is warehoused in Texas (or another US location) and ships directly to distributors, the Venezuela address is operationally irrelevant to the supply chain. The distributor's supply comes from the US. This is the cleanest outcome for distributor recruitment — no logistics complications, no sanctions questions, predictable shipping via standard freight channels.

*Implication: State this clearly in the discovery call. "All products ship from our US warehouse. Venezuela is our LATAM commercial team's base — your supply chain is US-origin."*

**Reality B — Products ship from Venezuela or transit through Venezuela.**

Venezuela is under broad US OFAC sanctions (Executive Order 13808 and subsequent). Most sanctions are targeted at specific individuals, the Venezuelan government, and PDVSA (the state oil company). Commercial goods trade between private Venezuelan companies and foreign distributors is generally permitted under a General License — but this is not universally understood by distributors or their banks.

If ELIMFILTERS® ships product from Venezuela, LATAM distributors (especially Colombian or Chilean distributors with US banking relationships) may face:
- Bank refusal to process payment to a Venezuelan entity
- Payment delays due to correspondent banking compliance reviews
- Questions from their own customers or investors about supply chain origin

*Implication: This must be resolved before recruitment. Options: (1) Shift shipment origin to a US or third-country warehouse, (2) Engage legal counsel to confirm the applicable General License and provide distributors with a sanctions compliance one-pager, (3) Accept that Venezuelan-origin supply will be a friction point and address it proactively in every commercial conversation.*

**Reality C — Products ship from a third location (e.g., contract manufacturer in Asia or Europe, with regional warehouse elsewhere in LATAM).**

If manufacturing is contracted in China, Southeast Asia, or Europe, and products are warehoused outside the US and Venezuela, the supply chain complexity is different. Lead times from an Asian manufacturer may be 45–90 days by sea, making the 30-day coverage requirement challenging unless a regional buffer warehouse exists.

*Implication: Define the regional warehouse strategy. A bonded warehouse or 3PL in a Colombian free trade zone, or a distribution center in Miami or Houston, can reduce effective lead times to 5–15 days from order placement.*

## 2.4 Available Options for Supply Chain Structure

---

### Option A — US-Origin Direct Shipping (Texas Warehouse)

Products are warehoused in the US (existing Texas HQ or a third-party logistics partner in Houston or Miami). All LATAM distributor orders ship from the US by sea freight (container or LCL for smaller orders) or air freight for urgent shipments.

**Estimated transit times:**

| Destination | Sea Freight | Air Freight | Notes |
|-------------|------------|------------|-------|
| Mexico (Manzanillo/Veracruz) | 7–12 days | 3–5 days | Short sea route; rail option for interior |
| Colombia (Barranquilla/Cartagena) | 10–18 days | 4–6 days | Standard Atlantic route |
| Chile (San Antonio/Valparaíso) | 22–35 days | 5–7 days | Longest LATAM sea route |

**Advantages:**
- No Venezuela logistics complications — US-origin supply is commercially standard
- Predictable shipping via established freight forwarders (Flexport, DHL, Maersk, etc.)
- Payment in USD, no currency risk to supply chain
- Clears customs as US-origin goods under most LATAM trade agreements

**Risks:**
- Sea freight to Chile takes 22–35 days — this makes the 30-day inventory coverage requirement tight unless Chilean distributors maintain 45-day coverage or ELIMFILTERS® holds a LATAM buffer stock
- Shipping cost per order is higher than regional shipping — opening orders of $6,000 USD may incur $400–$800 in freight costs (6–13%), which either erodes distributor margin or must be built into pricing
- For small restocking orders (sub-$3,000), freight cost as a percentage becomes uneconomical — distributor will be incentivized toward infrequent large orders rather than consistent coverage

**Freight cost guidance (estimated, subject to confirmation):**

| Route | Order Size | Estimated Freight | % of Order |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| Texas → Mexico | $6,000 | $350–$500 | 5–8% |
| Texas → Colombia | $6,000 | $450–$650 | 7–11% |
| Texas → Chile | $6,000 | $600–$900 | 10–15% |
| Texas → Chile | $15,000 | $700–$1,000 | 5–7% |

*Note: These are estimated ranges for LCL (less-than-container load) sea freight. Actual rates depend on current market conditions, Incoterms, and freight forwarder relationships.*

---

### Option B — LATAM Regional Hub (Colombia or Panama)

ELIMFILTERS® places buffer inventory at a regional warehouse or 3PL in Colombia (Bogotá or Barranquilla free trade zone) or Panama (Colón Free Trade Zone). Tier 1 LATAM distributors replenish from this regional hub. US inventory replenishes the hub on a monthly or bi-monthly consolidated shipment.

**Estimated transit times from regional hub:**

| Destination | From Bogotá | From Colón (Panama) | Notes |
|-------------|------------|-------------------|-------|
| Colombia (local) | 1–3 days | 5–8 days | Domestic delivery |
| Chile | 8–14 days | 7–12 days | Sea via Pacific |
| Mexico | 8–15 days | 4–7 days | Via Gulf/Caribbean |
| Peru | 6–10 days | 5–8 days | Pacific coast |

**Advantages:**
- All Tier 1 markets can receive inventory in under 15 days from a regional hub
- 30-day inventory coverage requirement becomes credible for all markets
- Smaller restocking orders become economical — short-haul freight is significantly cheaper than trans-Atlantic or US-LATAM routes
- No Venezuela supply chain questions from any distributor

**Risks:**
- Requires capital commitment: minimum buffer inventory of $30,000–$60,000 USD at wholesale (enough to service 4–6 distributors for one restocking cycle)
- Requires a warehouse agreement or 3PL relationship — administrative setup takes 4–8 weeks
- Currency and customs compliance for the hub country (Colombia: DIAN; Panama: ANA) must be managed
- If a hub distributor overstocks, the hub inventory cannot easily be redirected — careful allocation management required

**Colón Free Trade Zone (Panama) note:** The Colón FTZ is the largest free trade zone in the Western Hemisphere and the standard re-export hub for industrial goods entering Latin America. Many established industrial brands operate a Colón hub. Goods enter duty-free and are re-exported to LATAM countries. This is the most operationally established option for a regional hub.

---

### Option C — Hybrid: US-Origin for Mexico, Regional Hub for South America

Mexico ships direct from Texas (7–12 days sea, feasible). Colombia, Chile, and Peru ship from a regional hub (Colombia or Panama). This reduces the capital commitment compared to Option B (smaller hub, fewer markets) while solving the Chile lead-time problem.

**Advantages:**
- Mexico is the easiest market — short transit, high trade volume, established freight options
- Hub only needs to serve 2–3 South American distributors initially — lower capital commitment
- Scales naturally: add markets to hub as distributor network grows

**Risks:**
- Two-track logistics to manage (US-to-Mexico + US-to-Hub + Hub-to-South America)
- Freight forwarding relationship needed in two locations

---

### Option D — Air Freight for Opening Orders, Sea Freight for Restocking

Opening orders (the first $6,000 shipment to a new distributor) are sent by air freight regardless of destination. Speed of first delivery is a competitive priority — a distributor who receives their inventory in 5 days rather than 30 days begins selling 25 days sooner. Restocking orders use the appropriate sea freight route.

**Advantages:**
- First impression is powerful — fast delivery signals operational capability
- Air freight for a $6,000 opening order costs approximately $600–$1,200 (10–20% of order value) — high but one-time for the critical first shipment
- Restocking cadence (larger volumes, predictable timing) absorbs sea freight economics

**Risks:**
- Air freight cost on opening orders must be borne by someone — ELIMFILTERS® absorbs it (reduces program margin), distributor pays it (reduces their margin on first order), or it is split
- If ELIMFILTERS® absorbs air freight on opening orders, cost per new distributor activation is $600–$1,200 higher than projected
- Not a long-term supply chain strategy — only addresses the first-delivery problem

---

## 2.5 Recommended Approach

**Primary recommendation: Option C — Hybrid routing with immediate action on Venezuela clarification.**

The Venezuela question must be answered first, independently of the logistics structure decision. Before a supply chain option is chosen:

**Step 1 (this week): Determine the current supply chain reality.** Where are products currently warehoused? Where do they ship from? Does any portion of the supply chain run through Venezuela? This is a factual question that must be answered internally — it cannot be resolved by this document.

**Step 2 (based on the answer):**

- If supply currently originates from the US: adopt Option A for Mexico and negotiate 45-day inventory coverage for Chile rather than 30-day, to accommodate the sea freight timeline. Budget for a regional hub (Option B) by month 6 as distributor count grows.
- If supply currently runs through Venezuela: engage legal counsel to confirm OFAC compliance, and simultaneously begin the process of establishing a US-origin shipping capability or a regional hub as the primary distributor supply point. Do not launch active recruitment until alternative routing is established — the legal and commercial risk of a supply-chain-related discovery call answer that is "our product ships from Venezuela" is unmanageable.

**For Chile specifically:** The 22–35 day transit from Texas makes 30-day inventory coverage a policy mismatch. Adjust the Chile distributor agreement to require 45-day coverage rather than 30-day, explicitly in recognition of freight lead times.

# BLOCKER 3 — DISTRIBUTOR MARGIN ARCHITECTURE

## 3.1 Decision Required

**What wholesale margin does an authorized ELIMFILTERS® distributor earn on product purchases? How does that margin change as the distributor's annual purchase volume grows?**

This is the single most important commercial question a distributor will ask. It determines whether the financial economics of the program are attractive compared to competing brands. It must be answered before or during the first discovery call.

## 3.2 Context: What LATAM Industrial Distributors Expect

LATAM industrial distribution operates on margin economics that differ from North American norms. Key reference points:

**Commodity filtration brands (Fleetguard, Baldwin, WIX):** Standard distributor margins of 28–35% off MSRP. These brands compete on availability and price. Distributor margin is the primary incentive — the products sell themselves at the counter. Low technical support requirement. High price competition. Distributors typically sell multiple competing brands simultaneously.

**Premium/technical brands (Parker, Donaldson, Bosch Rexroth):** Standard distributor margins of 32–42% off MSRP, with volume tiers that increase margins as annual purchases grow. These brands compete on technical authority and application expertise. Distributor margin is competitive with commodity brands but the brand's technical infrastructure (application guides, cross-reference tools, sales support) reduces the effort required to close a sale. Distributors typically hold fewer brands and develop deeper expertise.

**Where ELIMFILTERS® should position:** Aligned with premium/technical brands. The Knowledge System, Part Search tool, ISO standards integration, and contamination control framework justify premium brand margin positioning. A distributor who cannot sell ELIMFILTERS® at a premium is either in the wrong market or has not been trained properly.

**LATAM distributor cost structure:** A LATAM industrial distributor operating profitably typically needs 35–45% gross margin on their portfolio to cover operating costs (salespeople, vehicles, warehouse, administration) and generate net profit. This means ELIMFILTERS® must provide a wholesale price that allows the distributor to reach 35–40% gross margin after freight, customs duty, and local handling costs.

**Customs duty consideration:** LATAM countries apply import duties to industrial filtration products. Typical rates:

| Country | Approximate Import Duty on Filtration Products | Notes |
|---------|-----------------------------------------------|-------|
| Mexico | 5–15% | USMCA reduces duties on US-origin products significantly |
| Colombia | 5–15% | Plus IVA (19%) on imported goods |
| Chile | 6% general rate | Chile FTAs reduce rates for many origins |
| Peru | 6–11% | Plus IGV (18%) |

These duties are paid by the distributor on the wholesale price they pay ELIMFILTERS®. They are a real cost that must be factored into the distributor's economics. A wholesale price that results in a 40% gross margin in Texas produces a 26–32% gross margin in Bogotá after 15% import duty + 19% IVA on the duty-paid value.

This is the primary reason margin architecture for LATAM distribution must account for landed cost, not just list price.

## 3.3 Available Options

---

### Option A — Single-Tier Flat Margin

One wholesale discount applies to all authorized distributors regardless of purchase volume. Suggested rate: 40% off MSRP.

**Example:** A distributor purchases a filter set with a combined MSRP of $10,000 USD. They pay ELIMFILTERS® $6,000 USD (40% discount). After 10% import duty ($600) and other landing costs (~$400), their total cost is approximately $7,000. They sell at MSRP ($10,000), earning a gross margin of 30% on the landed cost. After their selling expenses (salesperson commission, delivery, administration), net margin is approximately 15–20%.

**Advantages:**
- Simple to explain and administer — one number, no confusion
- No volume calculation or tracking required
- Treats all distributors equally — no competitive friction between distributors in adjacent markets

**Risks:**
- Provides no incentive for volume growth — a distributor buying $100,000/year earns the same margin as one buying $20,000/year
- May be below market for experienced distributors with negotiating power — a Tier 1 distributor in Mexico who currently earns 42% on a competing brand will not switch for 40%
- Provides no mechanism to reward top-performing distributors or incentivize restocking frequency

**Appropriate when:** Maximum simplicity is required for Version 1, with a commitment to revisit the margin structure at year-end.

---

### Option B — Two-Tier Volume-Based Margin

Two margin tiers based on annual purchase volume. Entry tier at onboarding; growth tier upon reaching a volume threshold.

**Suggested structure:**

| Tier | Annual Purchase Volume | Wholesale Discount |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Standard (entry) | < USD $36,000/year | 38% off MSRP |
| Growth | ≥ USD $36,000/year | 43% off MSRP |

Note: $36,000 is the annual performance threshold already defined in the exclusivity model (6× the $6,000 opening order). This aligns the volume threshold for margin improvement with the threshold for maintaining exclusivity — one consistent number for the distributor to manage against.

**Example at Standard tier:** $10,000 MSRP purchase → $6,200 wholesale cost → after 10% duty ($620) + landing ($400) → $7,220 landed cost → sell at $10,000 → 27.8% gross margin.

**Example at Growth tier:** $10,000 MSRP purchase → $5,700 wholesale cost → after 10% duty ($570) + landing ($400) → $6,670 landed cost → sell at $10,000 → 33.3% gross margin.

**Advantages:**
- Rewards volume growth with a material margin improvement (5 percentage points) — a genuine commercial incentive
- Aligns the margin threshold with the exclusivity threshold — distributors have one target number that governs two program benefits simultaneously
- Standard tier is still commercially viable; Growth tier is genuinely attractive
- Creates a natural conversation at the 12-month exclusivity review: "Your volume qualifies you for Growth tier pricing going forward"

**Risks:**
- Slightly more complex than Option A — requires annual purchase volume tracking
- Standard tier gross margin (27–28% after duties) may be tight for distributors in high-duty markets (Colombia, Peru) — those distributors may need the Growth tier economics to operate profitably
- Growth tier requires ELIMFILTERS® to absorb a margin reduction from 62% gross (at 38% distributor discount) to 57% gross (at 43% discount) — this must be sustainable at volume

**Appropriate when:** ELIMFILTERS® wants to incentivize distributor growth and align program economics with the exclusivity retention framework.

---

### Option C — Three-Tier Volume-Based Margin + Opening Order Bonus

Three margin tiers plus a one-time opening order incentive for new distributors.

**Suggested structure:**

| Tier | Annual Purchase Volume | Wholesale Discount |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|
| Launch (first 12 months) | Any | 41% off MSRP |
| Standard | < USD $36,000/year | 38% off MSRP |
| Growth | $36,000–$75,000/year | 43% off MSRP |
| Strategic | > USD $75,000/year | 46% off MSRP |

The Launch tier applies for the first 12 months of any distributor's program membership, regardless of volume. This gives every new distributor a better margin during the brand development phase — the period when their volume is lowest and their investment is highest. After 12 months, they transition to the tier that matches their volume.

**Advantages:**
- Launch tier removes the most common early-stage objection: "I can't profitably sell a new brand at startup volumes"
- Three tiers provide a visible growth pathway — distributors can see exactly what their economics look like if they grow to $50,000 and then $100,000 in annual purchases
- Strategic tier creates a genuine program for anchor distributors in large markets (Mexico City, Bogotá) who have the potential to build a significant ELIMFILTERS® business

**Risks:**
- Most complex margin architecture — requires accurate tracking of 12-month launch period and annual volume by distributor
- Launch tier margin (41%) means ELIMFILTERS® earns 59% gross before logistics and overhead during the first year — acceptable but lower than the Standard tier scenario
- Three tiers and a launch period create more negotiation surface area during discovery calls — distributors may try to negotiate custom rates or ask for extensions on the launch period
- $75,000/year threshold for Strategic tier requires a very active distributor — likely only reachable by 1–2 distributors in the first 3 years

**Appropriate when:** ELIMFILTERS® is prepared to manage program complexity and wants a full commercial framework communicated from day one.

---

### Option D — Margin on Application (Negotiated Case by Case)

No published margin structure. ELIMFILTERS® negotiates the wholesale discount individually with each distributor during the discovery call.

**Advantages:**
- Maximum flexibility — large distributors can be offered more; small distributors can be started at lower margins with a path to improvement
- No commitment to a specific number before pricing strategy is fully developed

**Risks:**
- Creates distributor-to-distributor inequity that becomes visible when distributors talk to each other (they will)
- Every discovery call becomes a margin negotiation — significant commercial team time and inconsistent outcomes
- Signals to sophisticated distributors that the program is immature — established brands have published program economics
- Almost certain to be discovered: a distributor in Medellín and a distributor in Santiago will eventually compare notes

**Appropriate when:** Only as a temporary bridge measure (maximum 30 days) while the structured option is finalized internally.

---

## 3.4 Recommended Approach

**Recommendation: Option B — Two-Tier Volume-Based Margin (38% Standard / 43% Growth), with one structural adjustment.**

The adjustment: use the Growth tier threshold of $36,000 annually to align with the existing exclusivity performance framework — this gives distributors one number to manage against (the threshold that governs both pricing and territory security).

**Why not Option C:** The three-tier structure is commercially superior in theory but premature for Version 1. ELIMFILTERS® does not yet have the administrative infrastructure to track launch-period timing and three volume tiers across 4–8 distributors. Complexity that cannot be executed consistently becomes a source of distributor disputes. Start with two tiers; add the Strategic tier at Year 2 when the top-performing distributors emerge.

**Why not Option A:** A flat 40% margin with no volume incentive is a missed opportunity. The exclusivity threshold ($36,000 annual) is already defined — building a margin improvement at the same threshold costs nothing to administer and meaningfully rewards distributors who perform.

**MSRP guidance:** For ELIMFILTERS® to be commercially viable, the MSRP (the price distributors charge their customers) must be set at a level that allows distributors to earn 30–35% gross margin after duties and landed cost at the Standard tier. This implies that the MSRP should be approximately 65–70% above the Standard tier wholesale price.

**Example for validation:**

```
Standard tier wholesale price:     USD $62 per unit (38% off MSRP of $100)
Distributor landed cost (Colombia):
  Wholesale:                        $62.00
  Import duty (12%):                 $7.44
  Freight + handling:                $4.50
  Total landed:                     $73.94
  
Distributor MSRP:                  $100.00
Gross margin on MSRP:              26.1%    ← Borderline for Colombia
Gross margin if sold at 10% premium: $110 → 32.8%  ← Workable

Growth tier wholesale price:       USD $57 per unit (43% off MSRP of $100)
Distributor landed cost (Colombia):
  Wholesale:                        $57.00
  Import duty (12%):                 $6.84
  Freight + handling:                $4.50
  Total landed:                     $68.34
  
Gross margin at MSRP:              31.7%    ← Viable
Gross margin at 10% premium:       37.9%    ← Healthy
```

**Conclusion from the example:** At the Standard tier, distributors in high-duty markets (Colombia, Peru) will need to sell at or above MSRP to operate with healthy margins. This is appropriate for a premium brand — distributors are not expected to discount. At the Growth tier, the economics are healthy at MSRP in all Tier 1 markets.

## 3.5 What to Tell Distributors Before Pricing Is Finalized

If this decision cannot be made before outreach begins, a holding statement can be used in discovery calls to acknowledge the question without committing to a number:

> "The wholesale discount for authorized distributors in our Tier 1 markets runs in the range of 38–43%, depending on annual purchase volume. We'll provide the full pricing schedule along with the program terms after the discovery call. What I can tell you is that we are positioned as a premium technical brand — we are designed to be sold at a price point that protects your margin, not one that puts you in a price war with commodity suppliers."

This statement is honest, communicates the range without committing to the exact structure, and reinforces the premium positioning argument.
