# ELIMFILTERS® Public AI Communication Rule

Status: Active communication governance
Effective: 2026-08-18

## Rule

In public technical-commercial communication, artificial intelligence must not be presented as a product auditor, autonomous quality authority, substitute for engineering personnel, or principal operating proposition of ELIMFILTERS.

Artificial intelligence may be referenced only as a mathematical and computational engineering tool used to support analysis of demanding or extreme operating conditions affecting protected assets, filtration systems, filtration media, and related material behavior.

## Permitted technical context

Where supported by the engineering work being described, mathematical or computational models may analyze interactions among variables such as:

- Contaminant loading
- Flow rate
- Pressure differential
- Temperature
- Filtration-media behavior
- Material response
- Service duration
- Operating severity
- Other application-specific variables with documented relevance

The purpose of this modeling is to support engineering assessment, sensitivity analysis, scenario evaluation, and physical-test planning.

## Mandatory limitation

Mathematical or computational modeling does not:

- Certify a product
- Replace physical validation
- Replace documented laboratory or production testing
- Establish a performance claim by itself
- Replace professional engineering judgment
- Transfer technical accountability to an automated system

## Commercial writing standard

Public ELIMFILTERS copy must remain technical-commercial in tone and demonstrate command of filtration engineering, manufacturing, validation, applications, asset operating conditions, distribution, and lifecycle economics.

The horizontal operating model must be explained through organizational efficiency, process standardization, disciplined data management, digital workflows, reduced administrative friction, and direct technical governance. Public copy must not attribute the commercial cost advantage to AI agents or present automation as the central value proposition.

The economic distinction remains structural rather than a low-price strategy. Engineering requirements, material specifications, manufacturing controls, validation requirements, and accountable technical governance are not reduced to obtain that efficiency.
