Filtration is not a product selection problem.
Filtration is a contamination control system problem.

Equipment reliability is determined by how effectively the total filtration system
controls contamination across all critical domains: air, fuel, hydraulic, cabin, lube, compressed air.

The decision hierarchy:
1. Understand contamination sources and targets
2. Apply relevant standards as measurement tools
3. Select technologies that control measured contamination
4. Implement via specific filtration products

Product selection is the LAST step, not the first.

## CONTAMINATION MODES

### Particle Contamination
- Sources: Ingested dust, wear debris, assembly contamination, seal degradation
- Target systems: All fluid systems
- Failure mechanism: Abrasive wear → clearance increase → efficiency loss → seizure
- Measurement: ISO 4406 cleanliness code (e.g., 17/15/12)
- Critical size: 4-14µm (matches bearing and valve clearances)

### Water Contamination
- Sources: Condensation, seal failure, coolant cross-contamination, atmospheric ingestion
- Target systems: Fuel, hydraulic, lube
- Failure mechanisms:
  - Fuel: Microbial growth, injector stiction, free water phase
  - Hydraulic: Cavitation, corrosion, additive depletion
  - Lube: Oil film breakdown, bearing corrosion
- Measurement: ASTM D6304, ISO 12937 (ppm water content)
- Critical threshold: >200ppm in hydraulic systems triggers accelerated degradation

### Varnish/Sludge
- Sources: Oil oxidation, thermal degradation, additive breakdown
- Target systems: Hydraulic, lube
- Failure mechanisms: Valve stiction, flow restriction, deposit buildup
- Detection: MPC (Membrane Patch Colorimetry), RULER analysis

### Silica (Dust Ingestion)
- Sources: Air intake bypass, damaged seals, poor maintenance
- Target systems: Engine cylinders, turbochargers
- Failure mechanism: Abrasive wear rate 100x higher than other particles
- Measurement: Spectrometric oil analysis (Si levels >10ppm indicates ingestion)

## FAILURE MECHANISMS — ENGINEERING DEFINITIONS

### Abrasive Wear
Three-body abrasion: hard particle (silica, iron oxide) + two metal surfaces
Result: Micro-cutting → surface removal → increased clearance → efficiency loss
Rate: Proportional to (particle hardness / surface hardness)² × contact stress

### Adhesive Wear
Metal-to-metal contact under boundary lubrication failure
Sequence: Oil film breakdown → asperity contact → micro-welding → surface tearing
Trigger: Temperature spike, contamination, incorrect viscosity

### Fatigue Wear
Cyclic stress below yield strength → subsurface crack propagation → spalling
Common in: Rolling element bearings, gear teeth
Accelerated by: Water in oil (reduces fatigue life 5-10x at 0.1% water content)

### Cavitation
Hydraulic: Pressure drop below fluid vapor pressure → bubble formation → implosion
Implosion pressure: Up to 60,000 psi at bubble collapse
Damage: Pitting on pump housings, valve surfaces, cylinder walls
